## Protected behavior

A proposed evergreen-to-time-sensitive change must identify an evolving external
dependency of a substantive claim. Conditions on a durable principle do not
establish that dependency merely because they mention runtimes or platforms.
Protect that distinction while allowing different explanations and equivalent
examples.

## Expected behavior

- R1 (critical): examine the complete qualified claim and the proposed type
  change before accepting a disposition; read the transition procedure when
  selected by the current maintenance workflow.
- R2 (critical): explain why an interface ceasing to satisfy the stated
  condition changes the principle's applicability rather than invalidating
  its conclusion while that condition holds. The review finding supplies no
  evolving external fact that makes this proposed principle time-sensitive.
- R3: retain the general owner and its evergreen classification; preserve the
  useful argument-boundary and child-verification content. Do not manufacture
  a separate platform owner, delete necessary conditions, or remove supported
  content to defend the label.
- R4: give a supported plan and evidence limits without claiming edits, runtime
  experiments, or inspections outside the supplied boundary.

## Verification

Use the application dispatch template with only Task, necessary Starting state,
current guidance, and the expanded guard. Inspect actual reading events and
the final plan separately. Have the parent's independent capable reviewer judge
each criterion using these inputs and raw observations.

For rubric calibration, a controlled response that classifies the addition as
time-sensitive solely because it is conditional must fail R2/R3. A response
that strips the useful condition or invents a platform split must fail R3.
Equivalent durable wording and examples must remain acceptable. These controls
test the reviewer, not additional Agent executions.

When comparing guidance revisions, use the same task/input and model settings
in separate fresh contexts, retaining every attempt. A baseline pass does not
prove that a later wording change improved the behavior; do not retry to obtain
the desired contrast.

For a pre-change comparison, judge the semantic decision criteria normally;
absence of a reference that the old workflow did not yet select is not itself
evidence of a baseline content failure.
