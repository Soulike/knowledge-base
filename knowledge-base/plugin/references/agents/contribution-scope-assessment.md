# Assess contribution scope

## Scope

Use this reference when forming a contribution candidate or assessing existing
content. It supports workflow decisions about evidence-backed claim scope,
reader needs, and organization across domains.

## When to update

Update this reference when a contribution or review case exposes a missing
distinction between source circumstances, supported claims, independent reader
needs, or appropriate organization.

## Assess each responsibility

Treat the source scenario as the origin of evidence. It does not predetermine
the claim's applicable range, readers, or final home. For each responsibility:

1. Separate concrete observations, the explanation or invariant supporting
   them, conditions necessary for that explanation to hold, and incidental
   circumstances of the source. Keep necessary conditions in the claim.
2. Actively investigate whether the explanation supports reader tasks outside
   the source scenario. Identify what those readers must understand or decide,
   their available context, and plausible existing owners. Test the proposed
   range using applicable authoritative sources, established mechanisms, and
   representative evidence. Similarity between scenarios alone establishes
   only a hypothesis; missing evidence does not establish generality.
3. Distinguish a supported broader claim, a claim that must retain its source
   qualifiers, and an unverified extension. Broaden only as far as the evidence
   supports, preserving applicability conditions and material uncertainty.
   Reject unsupported extensions as contribution claims without discarding a
   supported narrower responsibility.
4. Compare the supported reader needs with existing content and routes. Decide
   whether to retain, extend, split, merge, or move content from actual
   responsibilities, independently useful reading needs, and maintenance
   lifecycles. Broader applicability does not automatically require a general
   document or separation of general and specific material. Possible future
   reuse alone does not justify either operation.

When reviewing existing content, actively test whether its Scope, catalog
route, body, and source-specific limits conceal an independently needed
responsibility or impose an unsupported restriction. Use them as comparison
evidence, not as a ceiling on the analysis. Scope/body agreement alone does not
establish appropriate organization. Report an organizational defect only with
a concrete reader need, supporting evidence, and coherent remedy; an analogous
scenario or speculative reuse is insufficient.

Finish with the supported scope and conditions, evidence for retaining or
changing the organization, and unresolved hypotheses clearly distinguished.
These analytical conclusions do not expand the consuming workflow's authority
to edit, publish, or select review targets.
